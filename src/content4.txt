# Events & causation | ➝
## Event structure | ⇒
### States are locations | CT | Being in a state is being in a place.
- She's in trouble.
- He's out of danger.
- We're on the edge of a breakdown.
- We're in a mess.
- They're at war.
### Changes are movements | CT | To change is to move from one place to another.
- He went crazy.
- She came out of her coma.
- The light went from green to red.
- It turned into a mess.
### Causes are forces | CT | Causes push, pull and drive changes.
- The news drove her to tears.
- Hunger pushed them to steal.
- That pulled the country into war.
- It forced my hand.
### Actions are self-propelled movements | CT | Doing is moving on your own.
- We're moving ahead with the plan.
- She's making progress.
- He took a step toward a solution.
- They're going full speed.
### Purposes are destinations | CT | Goals are places to reach.
- She reached her goal.
- We're getting nowhere.
- He's on his way to a degree.
- We're almost there.
- That's a far cry from what we wanted.
### Means are paths | CT | The way you do something is the road you take.
- Do it this way.
- There's no way to get there from here.
- She went the long way around.
- He took a shortcut.
### Difficulties are impediments to motion | CT | Problems block, burden or slow movement.
- We hit a wall.
- She's bogged down in paperwork.
- He's got a lot of hurdles to clear.
- There's a roadblock.
- Let's get around this problem.
### Expected progress is a travel schedule | CT | Plans have a timetable you can be ahead of or behind.
- We're behind schedule.
- She's ahead of the pack.
- We're on track.
- He fell behind.
### Long-term purposeful activities are journeys | CT | Careers and projects are trips.
- Her career took off.
- He's at the crossroads of his career.
- The project is headed in the right direction.
- She's going places.
### Purposes are desired objects | PF | Achieving a goal is getting hold of something.
- She got the job.
- He's grasping for success.
- The prize slipped away.
- They have victory within reach.
### Attributes are possessions | CT | Properties are things one has.
- She has a lot of patience.
- He lost his temper.
- They have charm.
- She gained confidence.
### Activity is motion | WF | Being active is moving; stopping is standing still.
- Things are moving along.
- Business is at a standstill.
- She's on the go.
- The talks stalled.
### Activity is a journey | WF | Activities follow a path from start to finish.
- We're halfway through the project.
- She's starting out on a new job.
- We've reached the end of the season.
- Let's see it through.
### Events are objects | MW | Events are things located in time and space.
- The game is in the afternoon.
- I'll get through the meeting.
- The concert is behind us.
- We'll squeeze in the trip.
### An activity is a substance | MW | Activities have amounts and can be thin or rich.
- There was a lot of shouting at that meeting.
- I put a ton of energy into the garden.
- How much work do you have?
- I don't get much out of lectures.
### Events, actions and states are containers | MW | You are in a race, get out of a job, are in love.
- Are you in the marathon?
- I put a lot into that project.
- He's out of the running.
- She got out of jury duty.
## Causation | ⚙
### Causation is making | MW | To cause is to produce or manipulate something by hand.
- She made him cry.
- That drove him crazy.
- He brought about the change.
- The storm created chaos.
### Causation is emergence | MW | Effects come out of causes.
- His success came out of hard work.
- The war grew out of tensions.
- She emerged from poverty.
- That arose from a misunderstanding.
### Causation is a link | ML | Causes are tied to effects.
- There's a link between smoking and cancer.
- The two are connected.
- It sets off a chain reaction.
- They tied the crisis to the policy.
### Causes are paths | PF | A cause leads to its effect.
- That leads to trouble.
- Lying gets you nowhere.
- One thing led to another.
- That road leads to ruin.
### Causes are physical forces | PF | Events push other events.
- It pushed him over the edge.
- The vote tipped the balance.
- That set things in motion.
- Inflation is driving prices up.

# Quantity, value & status | ↑
## Up & down | ↕
### More is up; less is down | MW | Quantity rises like a pile; grounded in pouring water into a glass.
- Prices keep going up.
- Book sales rise every year.
- My income dropped.
- Turn the heat down.
- Sales hit a new high.
- That's a low figure.
### Good is up; bad is down | MW | Good things are high; bad things are low.
- Things are looking up.
- We hit a peak last year.
- It's been downhill since then.
- That's a high-quality product.
- Things are at an all-time low.
### Virtue is up; depravity is down | MW | Good people are upright and above things; bad ones sink.
- She's an upstanding citizen.
- That was a low trick.
- He has high standards.
- I wouldn't stoop to that.
- That was beneath you.
### High status is up; low status is down | MW | Power and rank are height.
- She rose to the top.
- He's at the bottom of the ladder.
- She's climbing the social ladder.
- He's fallen from grace.
- That's a lofty position.
### Having control or force is up; being subject to control is down | MW | Power is on top; the powerless are under.
- I have control over her.
- He's on top of the situation.
- She's under my command.
- He fell from power.
- They're the underdogs.
### Lack of control is down | WF | Losing control is falling.
- He fell apart.
- She was overcome.
- I fell for it.
- He went down hard.
### Finished is up | MW | Completing is bringing up.
- Drink up.
- I'll finish up.
- Wrap it up.
- Time's up.
### Mundane reality is down | MW | Everyday facts are on the ground.
- Come down to earth.
- He's got his feet on the ground.
- Things are at the ground level.
- Let's get down to business.
## Size & value | ◇
### Significant is big | MW | What matters is large.
- That's a big deal.
- Tomorrow is a big day.
- It's a small matter.
- He's a giant in his field.
- Those are minor problems.
### Important is central | WF | What matters is at the center; the trivial is on the edges.
- That's central to the argument.
- That's a peripheral concern.
- She's at the heart of it.
- That's a fringe view.
### Importance is weight | ML | Important things are heavy.
- That's a weighty matter.
- Her opinion carries weight.
- It's a lightweight argument.
- He threw his weight behind it.
### More is better | MW | Having more is good.
- We need more.
- He wants more out of life.
- The more the merrier.
- She's always wanting more.
### Bigger is better | MW | The large is better.
- Go big.
- That's a big win.
- He's a big shot.
- It's the biggest name in the industry.
### Smaller is better | MW | Coherence breaks: compact cars, tiny gadgets.
- It's lightweight and compact.
- Less is more.
- That model is sleeker.
- Small is beautiful.
### Similarity is closeness | PF | Similar things are near each other.
- Those colors are close.
- That's not even close.
- They're far apart on politics.
- It's near enough.
### Linear scales are paths | PF | Degrees on a scale are points on a road.
- She's far smarter than him.
- He's way ahead in the polls.
- That's a step up.
- It goes well beyond that.
### Difficulties are burdens | PF | Hard things are heavy to carry.
- She's weighed down by her duties.
- That's a load off my mind.
- He shouldered the blame.
- It's a heavy task.
### Help is support | PF | To help is to hold someone up.
- She supported me through it.
- Lean on me.
- He propped up the business.
- She has a strong support system.
### Organization is physical structure | PF | Abstract organization is the shape of a built object.
- The plan fell apart.
- How do the pieces fit together?
- The argument has a clear structure.
- The company is built around that idea.

# Things & containers | ▢
## Entities | ▪
### Inflation is an entity | MW | Inflation becomes a thing that can be measured and fought.
- Inflation is eating into wages.
- We need to combat inflation.
- Inflation is out of control.
- Inflation hit a new high.
### Inflation is an adversary | MW | Personified: inflation attacks, robs and has to be fought.
- Inflation is hammering families.
- Inflation stole my raise.
- Inflation is public enemy number one.
- Inflation caught the Fed off guard.
### The mind is an entity | MW | The mind is something that can be worked, broken or fixed.
- My mind isn't working.
- I'll have my mind made up by noon.
- Her mind is sharp.
- Get your mind right.
### Percepts are entities | WF | What is perceived becomes a thing.
- The sound came from the left.
- The smell filled the room.
- I caught a glimpse.
- There was a flash.
### Ideas are entities | WF | Ideas are things you can find and lose.
- I've got an idea.
- I lost my train of thought.
- Where did that idea come from?
- She found the answer.
### Personification | MW | Nonhuman things are given human motives and actions.
- Life has cheated me.
- The theory explains the results.
- Cancer finally caught up with him.
- The facts argue against it.
- My computer hates me.
### Wealth is a hidden object | MW | Money is found, dug for and hidden.
- He's seeking his fortune.
- She found a gold mine.
- He's digging for treasure.
- She struck it rich.
### Labor is a substance | MW | Work is stuff to be measured.
- How much work is left?
- There's a lot of labor in this.
- I put in a lot of effort.
- It needs more work.
## Containers | ▭
### Land areas are containers | MW | Territories have insides and outsides.
- He's out of Washington now.
- There's a lot of land in Kansas.
- Come into the field.
- She's deep in the forest.
### Substance is a container | MW | Water, air and crowds are things to be in.
- He's in the bathtub.
- Jump into the water.
- She's in the crowd.
- He disappeared into the fog.
### Classes are containers | WF | Categories are bounded regions; members are in or out.
- Whales are in the class of mammals.
- That's out of my category.
- She falls under that heading.
- It belongs in a different group.
### Categories are containers | PF | Things fall into or outside of kinds.
- That's outside the definition.
- It falls into the gray area.
- Put it in the 'maybe' pile.
- That's beyond the scope.
### Existence is location here | WF | To exist is to be present here.
- There's a problem.
- Here's an idea.
- There it is.
- Here we are.
### Nonexistence is location away | WF | To stop existing is to go away.
- The pain went away.
- The town disappeared.
- The bridge is gone.
- It vanished.
### Seeing a thing done stands for making sure it's done | WF | Watching over something is ensuring it.
- See that it gets done.
- I'll see to it.
- Watch that he doesn't fall.
- I'll look after the kids.

# Metonymy | ◑
## Metonymies | ◑
### The part for the whole | MW | A part stands for the whole thing or person.
- We could use some fresh faces.
- Move your butt.
- All hands on deck.
- That lab has some good heads.
- She's the brains of the team.
### Producer for product | MW | The maker stands for what was made.
- He bought a Ford.
- She owns a Picasso.
- I can't stand reading Kant.
- He's got a Rolex.
### Object used for user | MW | The tool stands for the one using it.
- The drums are late again.
- The trucks are on strike.
- The hired muscle wanted cash up front.
- We need a better bat in the lineup.
### Controller for controlled | MW | Who's in charge stands for what they control.
- Caesar crossed the Rubicon.
- Truman ended the war.
- A Tesla cut me off.
- The coach won the game.
### Institution for people responsible | MW | The organization stands for its members.
- Apple raised its prices.
- The Senate isn't convinced.
- The university will not budge.
- The army wants your sons.
### The place for the institution | MW | A building or city stands for who works there.
- The White House declined to comment.
- Washington doesn't listen.
- Wall Street is panicking.
- Hollywood loves a sequel.
### The place for the event | MW | Where something happened stands for what happened.
- This could turn into another Vietnam.
- Watergate changed politics.
- Remember the Alamo.
- It's another Pearl Harbor.
### The face for the person | MW | The face stands for who someone is.
- She's just a pretty face.
- There are a lot of new faces here.
- Put a face to the name.
- He's the face of the company.
### The physiological effects of an emotion stand for the emotion | WF | Red faces, shaking and tears mean feelings.
- He was red-faced.
- She was in tears.
- I had butterflies.
- Her hands were shaking.
### Things perceived stand for their percepts | WF | Naming the thing stands for experiencing it.
- I hear the piano.
- I smell coffee.
- I feel the wind.
- I see the stars.
### A member stands for the category | WF | Prototypes stand in for the whole category.
- A real mother stays home.
- He's a typical bird watcher.
- That's not a real bird.
- The paragon of the type.
### The stereotype stands for the category | WF | A social stereotype is taken as the whole kind.
- She's a working mother.
- He's a real man.
- Typical bachelor.
- What a housewife.
### Ideal for category | WF | The ideal member defines the category.
- That's the perfect husband.
- She's an ideal student.
- He's not a true scholar.
- An ideal world.
