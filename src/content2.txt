# Communication & argument | ❝
## Argument | ⚔
### Argument is war | MW | Arguments are won and lost; positions are attacked, defended and abandoned.
- Your claims are impossible to defend.
- She shot down every point I made.
- He attacked the weakest part of the case.
- Nobody wins an argument with her.
- Use that line and she'll destroy you.
- Go ahead, fire away.
- He retreated to a safer claim.
### Argument is struggle | MW | Before war there is wrestling: the child's tussle that becomes debate.
- We went back and forth for an hour.
- She wouldn't give an inch.
- He pinned me down on the details.
- I'm not going to fight you on this.
### An argument is a journey | MW | Arguments go somewhere, take steps and arrive at points.
- So far we've established three things.
- We've arrived at the crucial point.
- This brings us to the next issue.
- We've covered a lot of ground.
- Let's take it one step at a time.
- We set out to prove this.
### The path of an argument is a surface | MW | An argument covers ground and has content you can step on.
- We've covered most of the topic.
- That's well-trodden ground.
- We went over the details.
- There's a lot of ground left.
### An argument is a container | MW | Arguments hold content; they can be full, empty or have holes.
- Your argument has holes in it.
- There's not much in that essay.
- It's a hollow argument.
- That paper has a solid core.
- Can you put more into it?
### An argument is a building | MW | Arguments are framed, constructed, supported and toppled.
- The argument stands up.
- She laid the groundwork in chapter one.
- That claim is propping up the whole case.
- His reasoning collapsed under questioning.
### Rational argument is war | MW | Even polite academic debate keeps the battlefield logic.
- The paper is an attack on the standard view.
- She marshaled the evidence.
- He's on the defensive in the reviews.
- That objection is a real weapon.
### Argument is a dance | MW | An imagined culture where arguers move in balanced, pleasing steps.
- Let's take turns leading.
- That exchange was elegant.
- We fell into a rhythm.
- She stepped back to let him make the point.
## Communication | ✉
### The conduit metaphor | MW | Words are containers; meanings are objects packed in them and sent to a listener.
- I handed him the idea.
- I can't get it across to her.
- Your point came through loud and clear.
- Pack more into fewer words.
- The meaning is right there in the words.
- His words carry little meaning.
### Linguistic expressions are containers for meanings | MW | Sentences hold meaning; some are full, some are empty.
- Those are empty words.
- That sentence is loaded.
- There's no meaning in it.
- She reads too much into it.
### Communication is sending | MW | Talking is shipping a package from one head to another.
- I sent her a message.
- Did that get through?
- His point never reached them.
- I'm not receiving you.
### Words are weapons | ML | Speech wounds, cuts and fires.
- That remark hurt.
- She has a sharp tongue.
- He fired off an angry email.
- Those were cutting words.
### Listening is receiving | ML | Hearing is catching what is thrown.
- I didn't catch that.
- The message fell on deaf ears.
- She picked up on the hint.
- It went right past him.
## Discourse | ¶
### Discourse space is physical space | WF | Earlier talk is behind, coming talk is ahead, the point at hand is here.
- Here's the problem.
- There's the catch.
- As we saw above…
- In what follows…
### Discourse elements are entities | WF | Topics and points are things that sit somewhere and can be pointed at.
- Here's what I mean.
- That point stands.
- There goes my whole argument.
- Back to the main thread.
### The immediate future of a discourse is moving toward us | WF | What's coming next in talk is approaching the hearer.
- Here comes the good part.
- Coming up after the break.
- Wait, there's more coming.
- Here it comes.
### Nearest is first | MW | In word order, the closest or most important comes first.
- Me and Jim.
- Here and there.
- Now and then.
- This and that.
### More of form is more of content | MW | Longer words and repetition carry more meaning.
- He ran and ran and ran.
- It's very very big.
- She's tall-tall.
- Bigggg news.
### Closeness is strength of effect | MW | The nearer a word to what it modifies, the stronger its effect.
- I taught Sam French. (Sam learned it.)
- I taught French to Sam. (Maybe Sam didn't.)
- I found the bed soft. (I lay on it.)
- I found that the bed was soft. (I read a review.)

# Emotion | ✺
## Emotions in general | ✺
### The body is a container for the emotions | WF | Feelings fill the body, overflow and get let out.
- She was full of joy.
- He couldn't contain his excitement.
- Let it all out.
- I'm brimming over with pride.
- Get it out of your system.
### Physical and emotional states are entities within a person | MW | Moods and sicknesses are things inside us that come and go.
- He has a lot of rage in him.
- I can't get rid of this cold.
- The fear went right through me.
- Where did all that energy go?
### Emotions are bounded spaces | WF | Moods are places we fall into, fly into and are in.
- She flew into a panic.
- He's in a good mood.
- I'm in love.
- She fell into a depression.
- He got out of his funk.
### Emotional effect is physical contact | MW | Being moved is being hit, touched or knocked out.
- His speech touched me.
- I was struck by her kindness.
- The news hit him hard.
- That film really got to me.
- It knocked me off my feet.
### Emotions are forces | ML | Feelings sweep, drive and overwhelm.
- She was swept away.
- Jealousy drove him to it.
- I was overcome with grief.
- Fear held him back.
### Emotional states are weather | ML | Moods are sunny, stormy and cloudy.
- She has a sunny disposition.
- There's a storm brewing at home.
- He's been under a cloud all week.
- Her face clouded over.
### Existence is presence | WF | A feeling exists when it's here and stops when it leaves.
- My worry went away.
- The fear came back.
- The bad mood lingered all day.
- Her doubts vanished.
## Anger | ♨
### Anger is heat | WF | The most general metaphor for anger; heat applies to fluids or to solids.
- Don't get hot under the collar.
- He's a hothead.
- Things got heated.
- She's warming up to a fight.
### Anger is the heat of a fluid in a container | WF | The central anger metaphor: a body full of boiling liquid under pressure.
- I'm boiling over.
- Simmer down.
- She blew her top.
- He's just letting off steam.
- I'm about to explode.
- It made my blood boil.
- Keep a lid on it.
### Anger is fire | WF | Anger is something burning: smoldering, flaring up, put out.
- He was breathing fire.
- That just added fuel to it.
- Her temper flared.
- He's doing a slow burn.
- The quarrel was smoldering for weeks.
### Anger is insanity | WF | The angry person goes mad and loses reason.
- He went crazy when he heard.
- She was mad.
- I was out of my mind.
- He went berserk.
- She's raving.
### Anger is an opponent | WF | Anger is someone you struggle against and can lose to.
- I fought back my anger.
- He was seized by anger.
- She was overcome by fury.
- I wrestled with my temper.
### Anger is a dangerous animal | WF | Passions are beasts inside a person; anger bites and must be leashed.
- He unleashed his fury.
- Don't bite my head off.
- She has a ferocious temper.
- Keep your temper in check.
- He's in a bristly mood.
### Anger is a burden | WF | Anger is weight carried and set down.
- Get it off your chest.
- She carries that grudge everywhere.
- He has a chip on his shoulder.
- I felt lighter after yelling.
### The physiological effects of anger stand for anger | WF | Metonymy: heat, pressure, redness, shaking and blurred sight mean anger.
- He went red in the face.
- She was shaking with rage.
- I nearly burst a blood vessel.
- I couldn't see straight.
- He was blind with fury.
